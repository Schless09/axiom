# Axiom — 30-Day Go-to-Market Marketing Plan

**Goal:** Generate 3–5 qualified pilot conversations with VP Claims / CCO / Director of Claims decision-makers at regional TPAs, regional carriers, or self-insured fleet operators by Day 30.

**Guiding principle:** This is a founder-led sales motion. There is no brand yet, no case studies yet, and no marketing budget that outperforms a direct conversation. Every day-one activity should be designed to get a human on a call — not to build an audience.

---

## The Core Message (use this everywhere)

> *"Axiom runs three independent AI models against every piece of claim evidence — dashcam video, damage photos, police reports — and tells you where they agree, where they disagree, and when the score shouldn't be trusted. Statute-cited liability position in under five minutes. Shadow audit CSV in one click. Human adjuster makes the final call."*

**One-line version:**
> *"Three AI models. One liability scorecard. Built for the adjuster, not the demo."*

---

## Week 1 — Days 1–7: Foundation + First Outreach

**Theme: Get the tools in place and start the first 30 conversations.**

### Day 1 — Build the target list

- Build a spreadsheet of 50 target companies: regional TPAs, regional/super-regional carriers, large self-insured fleets, captive managers. Prioritize companies that have publicly mentioned dashcam programs, AI interest, or shadow audit requirements.
- Sources: LinkedIn Sales Navigator (title: VP Claims, Chief Claims Officer, Director of Claims, Claims Technology), industry directories (NAIIA, TMPAA, RIMS membership lists), conference speaker lists from Claims Journal, CLM Annual Conference, and RIMS.
- For each company, find: company name, target title, target name, LinkedIn URL, estimated annual claims volume if findable, any recent public statement about AI or dashcam adoption.
- Goal: 50 contacts, prioritized as Tier 1 (TPA, 50–500 employees, active dashcam program), Tier 2 (regional carrier), Tier 3 (fleet/captive).

### Day 2 — LinkedIn profile + company page

- Rewrite the founder LinkedIn headline: *"Building AI-powered liability review for auto insurance claims | Axiom"*
- Summary section: 3 short paragraphs. (1) The problem: dashcam footage sits unwatched, adjusters disagree on the same clip, leakage is invisible. (2) What Axiom does: three-model consensus, statute alignment, adjuster override layer. (3) What you're looking for: TPA and carrier claims leaders willing to run a shadow audit pilot.
- Create a LinkedIn company page for Axiom. Banner image with tagline. About section uses the core message above. Add the URL to the product.
- Post a first personal LinkedIn post: tell the founding story in 150–200 words. What did you see that made you build this? No product pitching — just the observation that started it. End with a question to drive comments: *"For those in claims operations — how does your team currently handle dashcam evidence review?"*

### Day 3 — One-pager and demo environment

- Write a one-page PDF (or clean Google Doc that can be exported): headline, the problem in 3 bullets, how Axiom works in 4 steps, 3 differentiators, the pilot offer, and contact. This is the leave-behind for every call.
- Set up a demo environment in the product with a sample claim pre-loaded (dashcam video, at least one scored analysis, the adjuster review layer visible). This is what you screen-share on every discovery call.
- Record a 3-minute Loom walkthrough of the demo environment. This becomes the follow-up asset after every outreach message.

### Day 4 — Outreach messaging

Write three outreach message templates:

**Template A — "The video backlog" hook** (for operations-focused buyers):
> *"Hi [Name] — I noticed [Company] has been expanding its dashcam program. One thing I hear from claims leaders is that the footage piles up faster than adjusters can review it. I built Axiom specifically for that problem — three AI models review the clip, cite the relevant statute, and produce a scorecard the adjuster can approve or override in minutes. Would a 20-minute walk-through make sense? Happy to share a short demo in the meantime."*

**Template B — "Shadow audit" hook** (for compliance-focused buyers):
> *"Hi [Name] — Shadow audit requirements are showing up in more TPA contracts. I built Axiom to make that prep a one-click export instead of a multi-day manual pull — the system tracks AI liability vs adjuster override on every claim automatically. I'd love to show you how it works against your current book. Worth 20 minutes?"*

**Template C — "Single-model AI risk" hook** (for technology-aware buyers):
> *"Hi [Name] — A lot of carriers are deploying one AI model for liability review and hoping it's right. The problem is there's no signal when it hallucinates. Axiom runs three models independently, then runs a fourth to check if they all watched the same video. When they disagree, the score is withheld — not guessed. I'd like to show you the difference. 20 minutes?"*

### Day 5 — Send first 15 outreach messages

- Send to the top 15 Tier 1 contacts via LinkedIn InMail or connection request + message.
- Personalize the first two sentences for each (mention the company's dashcam program, a recent conference they spoke at, a LinkedIn post they made, etc.).
- Use Template A or B based on their apparent focus.
- Log every send in the target list spreadsheet with date and template used.

### Day 6 — Industry presence: two community posts

- Post in LinkedIn groups: Claims Management Society, Insurance Professionals, RIMS members group. Title: *"How is your team handling dashcam evidence review at scale?"* — open-ended question, no product pitch. Reply to every comment personally.
- Post a second personal LinkedIn post: one of the "pain points that keep clients up at night" rewritten as a 150-word standalone observation. Example: *"Two senior adjusters watch the same dashcam clip. One says 40% insured fault. One says 70%. The settlement depends on who picked up the phone. This is not a training problem. It's a methodology problem."* No product mention in the body — only in a comment if asked.

### Day 7 — Review, refine, rest

- Check LinkedIn InMail response rates from Day 5.
- Reply to any comments on posts from Day 6.
- Refine the outreach templates based on what landed.
- Document what you've learned so far.

## Week 2 — Days 8–14: Content Engine + Deeper Outreach

**Theme: Give decision-makers a reason to trust you before they talk to you.**

### Day 8 — Send second 15 outreach messages

- Send to next 15 Tier 1 and Tier 2 contacts.
- Introduce Template C (single-model AI risk) to the more tech-forward targets.
- Follow up with anyone who opened the Day 5 messages but didn't reply (LinkedIn shows read receipts on InMail).

### Day 9 — LinkedIn article: the leakage piece

Write and publish a 600–800 word LinkedIn article. Working title: **"You know you have loss leakage. Here's why you can't find it."**

Structure:
1. The aggregate view is fine. The granular view is missing. Leakage shows up at the jurisdiction level, the adjuster level, the claim type level — not in the annual reserve review.
2. The core problem: AI vs adjuster delta is only computable if the AI scores every claim. If it scores half, the delta is noise.
3. What a real leakage dashboard looks like: 6-month trend, jurisdiction breakdown, high-variance claim cohort, one-click shadow audit export.
4. Close: *"If you're preparing for a shadow audit or want to benchmark what your AI tool is actually doing vs your adjusters, I'd like to show you what we've built."*

Do not write a press release. Write an opinion piece from operational experience.

### Day 10 — Engage the Claims Journal and CLM ecosystem

- Submit a contributed article pitch to Claims Journal (claimsjournal.com/contribute) or PropertyCasualty360. Proposed angle: *"Why three AI models beat one for dashcam liability review — and when none of them should score."* This is a 3–6 week lead time; start it now.
- Find the next CLM (Claims & Litigation Management Alliance) conference or webinar. Identify the speaker submission window. Proposed topic: *"AI-assisted liability review: consensus scoring, hallucination detection, and the adjuster override layer."*
- Connect on LinkedIn with 5 industry journalists/editors at Claims Journal, Insurance Journal, and Digital Insurance. No pitch yet — just connect with a note: *"I'm building in the claims tech space and enjoy your coverage."*

### Day 11 — The "four AI models" post

Personal LinkedIn post, 200 words. Working title: **"Every AI claims tool I've seen uses one model. Here's why that's a problem."**

Body: Explain in plain language what happens when one model hallucinates on a dashcam clip — it gives a number, the adjuster trusts it, the claim closes. No one knows. Now explain what happens when three models disagree by 30 percentage points — you know something is wrong before the adjuster touches it. Close: *"That's why Axiom runs four. Three to analyze, one to check if the first three watched the same video."*

This is designed to travel. Tag it with: #insurtech #claimstech #AI #TPAs.

### Day 12 — Send follow-ups to Week 1 outreach

- Send a first follow-up to the 30 contacts who haven't replied from Days 5 and 8.
- Follow-up message is shorter: *"Hi [Name] — following up on my note last week. I recorded a 3-minute walkthrough of how Axiom handles dashcam review — happy to share if useful. Otherwise, no worries."* Attach or link the Loom.
- Log responses. Anyone who replies gets moved to "active conversation" in the tracker.

### Day 13 — Target the adjacent buyer: risk managers

- Run a separate LinkedIn search for Risk Manager, Head of Risk, Fleet Risk Manager at companies with 500+ vehicle fleets (logistics, trucking, utilities, municipalities).
- Write a fourth outreach template for this audience focused on fleet exposure and dashcam proliferation rather than TPA workflow.
- Send to 10 fleet/captive targets.

### Day 14 — Compile week 2 learnings

- How many replies total? What objections are coming up?
- What messaging is resonating?
- What questions are prospects asking that you don't have a good answer to?
- Update the one-pager and demo script based on these answers.

## Week 3 — Days 15–21: Conversations + Proof Points

**Theme: Convert interest to booked calls. Start building the first proof points.**

### Day 15 — Book the first demo calls

- Anyone who has replied positively should be in the process of scheduling a call by now.
- Use Calendly or a simple email with 3 time slots. 20–30 minutes. Frame it as: *"I'll show you the platform on a live claim for 15 minutes, then I want to understand your current evidence review workflow for the last 10."*
- Send a confirmation email with: the Loom walkthrough link (so they come prepared), one paragraph on the pilot structure, and the one-pager attached.

### Day 16 — Discovery call prep and debrief template

Write a one-page discovery call script:
- What does their current evidence review workflow look like? (Who reviews dashcam? How long does it take? How many claims per week have video?)
- Do they have a dashcam mandate from their carrier clients, or is it self-imposed?
- Are they subject to shadow audit requirements? When is the next one?
- Have they tried any AI tools? What happened?
- What would a successful pilot look like to them?

Write a post-call debrief template to fill in after every call: contact info, company size, claim volume, current workflow, key pain point, objections raised, next step agreed.

### Day 17 — Reddit and Slack outreach (low volume, high quality)

- Find the relevant communities: r/Insurance, r/InsuranceProfessional, Claims & Litigation Management Alliance Slack or Discord if it exists, RIMS Connect community.
- Do not pitch. Introduce yourself and ask a genuine question: *"I'm building in the claims tech space and trying to understand how TPAs are currently handling dashcam review at scale. Anyone willing to share their workflow?"*
- This generates qualitative research and occasionally surfaces warm leads.

### Day 18 — LinkedIn article: the statute alignment piece

Write and publish a 500–700 word LinkedIn article. Working title: **"The quiet problem with AI liability scoring: it can cite a statute that doesn't exist."**

Body: Explain how generative AI hallucinates citations. Explain how the alternative — a jurisdiction-specific statute database with deterministic matching from controlled vocabulary tags — prevents this. Close with the implication for litigation: *"If your AI cited a statute in the claim file and defense counsel pulls it up and finds it doesn't exist, you have a bigger problem than leakage."*

### Day 19 — Send third 10 outreach messages

- Send to the remaining Tier 2 and Tier 3 targets.
- Personalize using any recent LinkedIn activity (posts, job changes, conference appearances).
- By now you have real signal on which templates are working. Use the highest-performing one.

### Day 20 — Second follow-up to non-responders

- Anyone who hasn't replied to two messages gets one final note: *"Hi [Name] — I'll leave it here. If the timing ever makes sense to look at AI-assisted liability review with a shadow audit pilot structure, I'd be glad to connect. Good luck with the book."*
- This is designed to close the loop gracefully. Occasionally it reopens the conversation.

### Day 21 — Compile call transcripts and objection map

- After any discovery calls you've had by now, write down every objection you heard.
- Common expected objections: *"We already have an AI tool."* / *"Our adjusters don't trust AI."* / *"What does integration look like?"* / *"How do we know the output is accurate?"*
- Write a crisp one-sentence answer to each. These become the FAQ section of the website and the objection-handling guide for future sales conversations.

## Week 4 — Days 22–30: Pilot Proposal + Channel Setup

**Theme: Convert the best conversations into pilot agreements. Build the channel that will outlast the 30 days.**

### Day 22 — Write the pilot proposal template

The pilot proposal is a 2-page document (not a deck):

**Page 1:**
- What we're proposing: a 60-day shadow audit pilot covering [X] claims per month
- What the client does: upload evidence as normal; adjusters continue their existing workflow
- What Axiom does: analyze every uploaded claim, produce a liability scorecard per item, track AI-vs-adjuster delta automatically
- What the client gets at the end: a shadow audit report showing agreement rate, average delta, jurisdiction breakdown, and financial opportunity estimate

**Page 2:**
- Pilot fee: [X] flat for 60 days, covering up to [Y] claims
- Success criteria (agreed in advance): what agreement rate would constitute a successful pilot?
- Path to production: what does the full deployment look like after the pilot?
- Data terms: org-scoped, encrypted, no data used for training without consent, deletion on request

Customize the claim volume and fee for each prospect based on their book size.

### Day 23 — Start a newsletter (low commitment)

Create a LinkedIn newsletter called **"Claims Intelligence"** — a biweekly newsletter for claims leaders on AI, evidence review, leakage, and liability technology. First issue: 300-word summary of the two LinkedIn articles from Weeks 2 and 3. Invite your network to subscribe.

This is a long-term asset. It builds a subscriber list of your exact buyer profile over time.

### Day 24 — Identify two industry events to attend or sponsor

- CLM Annual Conference (Spring): premier claims leadership event. Booth or speaker slot.
- TMPAA Annual Meeting (Fall): TPA-specific executive audience. Sponsorship tier is accessible at early stage.
- RIMS Annual (Spring): risk manager audience, fleet and captive buyers.
- Insurance Analytics USA: data and AI-focused insurance audience.

Register or submit a speaker proposal for at least one. Conference networking generates warm conversations at a density impossible to replicate with cold outreach.

### Day 25 — Reach out to claims consultants and MGAs

- Claims management consultants (firms that advise carriers and TPAs on operations) are potential channel partners — they recommend tools to their clients.
- MGAs (Managing General Agents) that write auto liability programs often have influence over TPA selection.
- Send 5 outreach messages to claims consultants on LinkedIn: *"I'm building claims tech and exploring whether there are partnership conversations worth having. Would love to hear how you advise clients on evidence review workflows."*

### Day 26 — Build a simple public-facing website or landing page

If the product URL is not yet public-facing, build a single landing page:
- Headline: *"A liability score on every claim. In minutes."*
- Subhead: *"Three AI models. One statute-cited scorecard. Human adjuster makes the final call."*
- Four capability bullets (video analysis, multi-model consensus, statute alignment, leakage monitoring)
- One CTA: *"Request a pilot walkthrough"* → email capture or Calendly link
- No pricing. No blog. Just enough to pass the credibility check when a prospect Googles the company name after receiving outreach.

### Day 27 — Post a case study teaser (even without a live client)

If no pilot client has been onboarded yet, write a "hypothetical walkthrough" post on LinkedIn:

*"Here's what Axiom's output looks like on a dashcam clip of a left-turn disputed liability claim."*

Walk through the scorecard: timeline events, statute match, three-model confidence levels, one model flagged for scene coherence drift, liability score withheld pending adjuster review. Use a real clip from a public source (YouTube dashcam footage clearly labeled as such) or a test clip. Be transparent that it's a demo, not a client claim.

This is product evidence. It shows the work without needing a live case study.

### Day 28 — Send pilot proposals to the warmest conversations

- Any prospect who has had a discovery call and expressed interest should receive a pilot proposal by Day 28.
- Send via email with subject: *"Axiom pilot proposal — [Company Name]"*
- Follow up the same day with a LinkedIn message: *"Sent you a note via email — let me know if you have questions or want to adjust the scope."*

### Day 29 — Second newsletter issue + LinkedIn recap post

- Publish the second "Claims Intelligence" newsletter issue: short takes on 2–3 topics relevant to claims leaders this month (dashcam proliferation trends, shadow audit requirements in TPA contracts, a link to a relevant industry article with your commentary).
- Post a personal LinkedIn recap: *"30 days of building Axiom. Here's what I've learned about how claims leaders think about AI."* 3–5 observations from the conversations you've had. No pitching — this is credibility-building for the next 30 days.

### Day 30 — Review and set 60-day plan

**Measure:**
- Total outreach messages sent
- Reply rate by template
- Discovery calls booked and held
- Pilot proposals sent
- Pilot agreements signed or verbally committed
- LinkedIn follower growth and post engagement
- Newsletter subscribers

**Define success for Day 30:**
- 3+ discovery calls held
- 1+ pilot proposal sent
- 1 pilot agreement in discussion or signed

**Set the 60-day plan:**
- Double down on the messaging that generated replies
- Schedule the conference or event identified on Day 24
- Begin content around the first proof point (even a partial one) as soon as it exists
- Start building the second tier of outreach (fleet risk managers, captive managers, MGA underwriters)

## What Not to Do in 30 Days

- **Do not run paid ads.** There is no targeting precision for this buyer profile in paid channels that competes with direct outreach. Save ad budget for after you have a case study.
- **Do not build the blog before building the prospect list.** SEO has a 6–12 month lag. Direct outreach has a 6–12 minute lag.
- **Do not pitch in LinkedIn comments on other people's posts.** Engage genuinely. The visibility is the value, not the product mention.
- **Do not wait for the website to be perfect.** A Calendly link and a Loom are enough to book a call.
- **Do not over-engineer the one-pager.** Two pages of clear copy outperforms a 20-slide deck in every B2B sales context.
- **Do not pitch a full deployment.** Pitch a pilot. The goal is a low-friction yes — not a signed enterprise contract in 30 days.
